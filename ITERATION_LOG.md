# What went wrong and what I changed

My first few tries at making a real call failed for setup reasons. Twilio blocked the call with a "No International Permission" error, which I fixed in its voice geo-permissions. Then LiveKit said the phone server needed a username and password, so I added the login from my Twilio credential list to the trunk. After that the call connected, but the bot said nothing. My OpenAI key in the `.env` file had been pasted across several lines, so OpenAI rejected it. I retyped it on one line and the bot started talking. At that point it just chatted about random things, so I replaced my first prompt with a patient who has a goal, like booking an appointment.

The next problem was speed. At first the bot took about 5 to 6 seconds to reply, because OpenAI was doing the listening, the thinking, and the talking. I switched the listening to Deepgram and the voice to Cartesia, and I turned on two settings that let the bot start its reply sooner. That brought the wait down to about 2 seconds. Some of my calls were also choppy, and I first blamed my settings. I tried changing the interruption settings, but I couldn't hear any difference. The real cause was my phone hotspot, which kept dropping the connection to LiveKit. On a stable connection the calls sounded clean.

I also made a mistake of my own. I started nine calls at once, so they overlapped and the recordings and transcripts got mixed up. I put that whole batch aside and redid every call one at a time. While reading the new transcripts, I found problems in my own bot. It refused to say its name when asked, made up a date correction nobody asked for, and talked over the greeting. I rewrote its instructions to fix each one. I also raised the call time limit from 4 minutes to 7, because one call got cut off in the middle of a sentence. A few calls were too short to be useful, like the insurance and refill calls, which ended in under a minute. I rewrote those scenarios so the bot asks follow-up questions, and then I redid the calls.

Near the end, my Cartesia credits ran out. The last two calls, unusual and schedule, use OpenAI's voice instead, so they sound different from the first eight.
